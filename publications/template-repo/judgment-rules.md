# Judgment Rules

This is a canonical MCAP file.

## Purpose

Use this file to preserve the human-owned criteria used to evaluate outputs, decisions, risks, claims, and AI assistance.

Judgment rules prevent AI fluency from becoming synthetic authority. They keep responsibility visible and reviewable.

## Core Judgment Rules

Rules that should guide future work:

- preserve accuracy over fluency
- distinguish evidence from interpretation
- state uncertainty when evidence is incomplete
- avoid unsupported claims
- preserve human responsibility for consequential decisions
- do not treat AI-generated material as verified without review

## Authority Boundaries

AI may assist with:

- summarizing materials
- comparing options
- identifying risks
- drafting alternatives
- checking consistency
- asking clarification questions

AI must not decide:

- final acceptance of claims
- ethical trade-offs
- legal conclusions
- medical or safety-critical actions
- publication approval
- irreversible project decisions
- changes to durable cognitive asset records without explicit human approval

## Claims Requiring Verification

Claims requiring source verification:

- empirical claims
- legal, medical, financial, or safety claims
- historical claims
- claims about people or organizations
- claims generated or summarized by AI

## Escalation and Exit Rules

Stop or escalate to human review when:

- context is missing
- authority boundaries are unclear
- evidence is insufficient
- the task affects legal, ethical, financial, medical, or safety outcomes
- the output may expose sensitive information
- the action is irreversible or difficult to reverse
- the instruction conflicts with these judgment rules

## Responsibility Rules

The human owner remains responsible for:

- final judgment
- publication decisions
- acceptance or rejection of AI suggestions
- verification of factual claims
- treatment of sensitive information
- decisions involving real-world consequences

## What Should Not Go Here

Do not include rules that delegate final responsibility to AI, vague preferences that cannot guide future review, expert advice beyond the user's authority, or instructions that conflict with privacy, safety, legal, or confidentiality obligations.
